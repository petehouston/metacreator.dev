Good Instagram bio ideas all solve the same problem: Instagram gives you 150 characters
for a bio
([Instagram Help](https://help.instagram.com/728994388226960/)), and it is the only text
a stranger reads before deciding whether to follow you. Here is the template, then
examples, then what to cut.

## The template

```text
[What you post] for [who it is for].
[The proof, or the differentiator, in a few words.]
[One instruction, if you have one.]
```

Three lines, well under the limit, and every one doing work:

```text
Home espresso, tested properly.
Every recommendation used for a month first.
New teardown every Tuesday ↓
```

## Instagram bio ideas by account type

**Creator or educator**
```text
I explain [subject] without the jargon.
[Credential or proof.]
New [format] every [day].
```

**Local business**
```text
[What you sell] in [place].
[The thing that makes it different.]
Open [hours] · Order below
```

**Portfolio or freelancer**
```text
[Discipline] for [client type].
Previously: [two recognisable names].
Booking [season] — DM
```

**Personal account with an angle**
```text
[Thing you do] · [thing you also do]
Documenting [the specific project].
```

The pattern is the same throughout: a noun that says what this account is, a phrase that
makes it credible, and at most one instruction.

## Writing one from nothing

If the template is not enough to start with, answer these three questions in a notes app
and then cut the answers down.

**What would somebody miss if this account disappeared?** That is what you post. Not your
job title, not your interests - the thing that would leave a gap.

**Why should they believe you?** A credential, a track record, a constraint you work
under. "Ten years in kitchens" and "every recipe tested three times" both do this job.
"Passionate about food" does not.

**What do you want them to do?** Follow is the default and does not need saying. If
there is something else - a newsletter, a shop, a booking form - say it once, plainly.

Now cut. The first draft is always over 150 characters, and the cutting is where the bio
gets good: what survives is what mattered.

## What to cut

- **Adjectives about yourself.** "Passionate", "creative", "dedicated". They are
  unfalsifiable and every account has them.
- **Emoji standing in for words.** One or two as punctuation is fine. A row of them as a
  substitute for a sentence makes the bio unreadable to a screen reader and unclear to
  everyone else.
- **Your own name, if it is already your display name.** It is directly above the bio.
- **Hashtags.** Clickable, but they send visitors away from the page where they were
  deciding to follow you, and they do not make your profile discoverable —
  [Instagram hashtags](/blog/instagram-hashtags).
- **"DM for collabs"** on an account with 400 followers. It reads as hopeful rather
  than open for business.

## The name field is not the bio

The display name above the bio is searchable in a way the bio is not, which makes it
prime real estate. An account called "Sam" is findable by people who know Sam. An account
called "Sam · Home Espresso" is findable by people looking for the subject.

That is one of the few genuine discovery levers on an Instagram profile, and most
accounts leave it empty of information.

## Styled text, carefully

Unicode styled text — the "fancy fonts" you see in bios — works by substituting different
Unicode characters for ordinary letters. It renders as decorative text, and screen
readers announce it as gibberish or skip it.

Use it for a word, not a sentence, and never for the line that carries your meaning. The
detail, including which styles survive being pasted, is in

:::faq
Q: How many characters is an Instagram bio?
A: 150. Line breaks count, so a three-line bio has less room than it looks.
Q: What should an Instagram bio include?
A: What you post, who it is for, one piece of proof, and at most one instruction. Links
and contact go in the dedicated fields.
Q: Do hashtags in a bio help?
A: They are clickable but do not place your profile in hashtag feeds, and they send
visitors away at the moment they were deciding to follow you.
Q: Should I use fancy fonts in my Instagram bio?
A: Sparingly. Unicode styled text is announced badly by screen readers, so keep the
meaningful line in ordinary characters.
:::
