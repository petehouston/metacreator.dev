Instagram allows up to 30 tags on a post
([Instagram Help](https://help.instagram.com/351460621611097)). Using all 30 is not a
strategy — it is a signal that you do not know which ones matter. Three to eight
relevant Instagram hashtags, mixed across reach levels, is what actually performs.

## How many Instagram hashtags to use

| | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Documented limit | 30 tags per post |
| Useful number | 3-8 |
| Where | Caption or first comment — no measurable difference |
| Bio hashtags | Clickable, but do not make your profile discoverable |

The gap between 30 and eight is the whole subject. Instagram's recommendation system
has spent years getting better at understanding what a post is *about* from its image,
caption and audio. Hashtags are now one input among many rather than the index they
were when the limit was set.

## Why the mix matters more than the count

A hashtag feed is ordered, and a post with modest engagement cannot compete in a tag
with millions of posts. It can compete in a tag with tens of thousands.

- **Broad tags** (`#coffee`) — millions of posts. Your post is visible for seconds.
  Worth one or two for categorisation.
- **Mid tags** (`#homeespresso`) — tens to hundreds of thousands. This is where a
  normal post can actually be seen and stay visible.
- **Niche tags** (`#lelitbianca`) — thousands. Small audience, high relevance, real
  engagement.

Aim the majority of your set at the middle tier. That is the only tier where the maths
works for an account that is not already large.

## Placement: caption or first comment

This argument has consumed an unreasonable amount of creator attention. There is no
measurable difference in reach. Choose on aesthetics: tags in the caption are visible
and read as clutter to some audiences; tags in the first comment keep the caption
clean.

What does matter is that the caption itself works. The fold lands early, and hashtags
at the top of a caption push your actual first line out of view —
[Instagram caption length](/blog/instagram-caption-length).

## What hashtags will not do

**They will not save a post nobody engages with.** Reach on Instagram is driven by how
people respond in the first hour. Tags help a post find a slightly wider audience;
they do not manufacture interest.

**They will not make your profile discoverable.** Hashtags in a bio are clickable but
do not put your profile into those feeds — the profile discovery levers are your name
field and your bio text:
[Instagram bio ideas](/blog/instagram-bio-ideas).

**They will not fix the wrong format.** A landscape image in a vertical feed is a
bigger handicap than any tag set can offset —

## Finding the mid-tier tags for your subject

The tier that matters is the hardest to find, because Instagram does not publish post
counts in a form you can sort. Three methods that work without a paid tool:

**Search the tag in the app.** The tag search shows post counts. Anything in the tens
to low hundreds of thousands is the range you want; millions is too crowded and a few
hundred is usually dead.

**Read what similar accounts use.** Not to copy the set wholesale, but to find the
vocabulary your niche has settled on. Communities converge on particular tags and an
outsider rarely guesses them.

**Follow the related tags.** Opening a tag shows related ones, which is the fastest way
from a broad tag you already know to the mid-sized ones you do not.

What none of these give you is a reliable difficulty score - Instagram does not expose
one, and any tool claiming a precise number is modelling rather than measuring. Post
count plus your own results is the honest instrument.

## Tags that quietly do nothing

Some tags are restricted, some are so generic they are effectively noise, and some are
simply dead. Nothing tells you which — the symptom is a post that never appears under a
tag you used. The diagnostic sequence is in
[banned hashtags](/blog/banned-hashtags).

The cross-network comparison, because none of this transfers to YouTube or LinkedIn, is
in [how to use hashtags](/blog/how-to-use-hashtags).

:::faq
Q: How many hashtags should I use on Instagram?
A: Three to eight relevant ones, weighted toward mid-sized tags. The 30 limit is a cap,
not a target.
Q: Do hashtags still work on Instagram?
A: As a topic signal and a browsable surface, modestly. They are no longer the main
route to reach - engagement in the first hour is.
Q: Should Instagram hashtags go in the caption or the first comment?
A: Either. There is no measurable reach difference, so decide on how you want the
caption to read.
Q: Do hashtags in my Instagram bio help people find me?
A: They are clickable, but they do not place your profile in those hashtag feeds.
:::
