The YouTube title length limit is 100 characters
([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/57407)) — but that number
is not the one that matters. Search results, suggested rails, the mobile app and the
watch page each cut a title at a different, shorter point, and the cut is where your
promise either survives or disappears.

## The YouTube title length limit, and where it does not apply

One hundred characters is what Studio will accept. What a viewer sees depends
entirely on where they see it:

| Surface | Roughly what survives | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Desktop search results | About 70 characters | Wide column, two lines |
| Suggested / sidebar | About 40-50 characters | Narrow column, two lines at small type |
| Mobile home and search | About 40-50 characters | Narrow screen, two lines |
| Watch page (desktop) | Nearly all of it | The one place the full title shows |

Those are approximations on purpose. YouTube renders titles in a proportional
typeface, so the cut is decided by pixel width rather than a character count — the
word "illiterate" occupies about half the width of "WOMAN" at the same character
count. Any guide that gives you one exact number for all surfaces is measuring the
wrong thing.

## The rule that follows from this

**Everything load-bearing goes in the first 40 characters.** Not the first 70 —
40, because the narrow surfaces are where most impressions happen and they are the
least forgiving.

That means the searchable phrase leads, the qualifier follows, and the flourish comes
last where losing it costs nothing:

- ❌ *"I Spent 30 Days Doing Something Unusual — Here Is What Happened To My Sourdough Starter"*
- ✅ *"Sourdough Starter: 30 Days, Two Failures, One Fix"*

The second one still reads as a title on the watch page and still works as a stub on
a phone. The first becomes "I Spent 30 Days Doing Something Unusual —".

## Why front-loading also helps discovery

The title is the strongest textual signal YouTube has about a video's subject, and it
is what a searcher scans. Both readers — the ranking system and the human — give more
weight to the front. That is not a trick; it is how scanning works, and it happens to
align with how the text is indexed.

The corollary is that a title beginning with your channel name, a series number or a
bracketed prefix spends its most valuable characters on something the viewer already
knows or does not care about. `[Ep. 47]` at the front costs you eight characters of
the forty that count.

## Where the title sits in the wider picture

Title, thumbnail and the first lines of the description work as one unit. The title
sets the query, the thumbnail earns the click, and the description keeps the promise.
Optimising one in isolation is how a video ends up with a high impression count and a
click rate that embarrasses it.

A title is read next to its thumbnail at sidebar size, not at full width. If the two

## Editing a title after publishing

Safe, and often the highest-return edit available on an existing video. A video with
watch history and a bad title is a video that is being under-shown; fixing the title
lets YouTube retest it. Two cautions:

- Change it once and give it time. Repeated edits inside a few days make the result
  unreadable, because you never see one version's performance cleanly.
- Do not change the meaning to chase a different query. A title that promises
  something the video does not deliver gets clicked and abandoned, and abandonment is
  the one signal that genuinely damages a video.

:::faq
Q: What is the YouTube title character limit?
A: 100 characters. YouTube Studio will not accept more, and titles are cut well
before that in most places a viewer sees them.
Q: How long should a YouTube title be?
A: Long enough to carry the query and the promise, with the important half inside the
first 40 characters. Somewhere between 45 and 70 characters suits most videos.
Q: Do emoji count toward the YouTube title limit?
A: Yes, and some count as more than one character depending on how they are encoded.
They also take up more visual width than a letter, so they eat into the truncation
point faster than the character count suggests.
Q: Does changing the title affect ranking?
A: It lets YouTube re-evaluate the video against different queries. That is usually
an improvement for a poorly titled video and a risk only if you change what the title
promises.
:::
