## Where free is genuinely enough

For everything above, yes. These are computations over your own inputs — resizing, counting
pixels, applying a formula, reading a public page — and nothing about them costs money to
run.

Where free stops being enough is anywhere a tool has to buy data or compute: search-volume
databases, competitor tracking over time, sentiment analysis across thousands of comments,
video transcoding at scale. If a tool claims to give you search volumes for nothing, it is
either modelling them or funding itself another way.

## The ones worth having open while you work

A short list, because a bookmarks folder of forty tools is a folder nobody opens.

**One counter.** Open while you write anything with a limit, which is everything.

**One resizer.** Open when you export.

**One preview.** Open before you publish something with a link in it.

That is three tabs and it catches the overwhelming majority of avoidable mistakes -
truncated titles, wrongly cropped images, and link cards showing last year's header
graphic. Everything else in this list is occasional: a calculator when a brand asks for a
number, an availability check when you are naming something new.

The reason to keep the list short is the same reason to keep a content calendar simple.
Tooling that requires maintenance stops being used, and a tool you do not open is worth
exactly nothing regardless of how good it is.

## What no tool fixes

Worth saying at the end of a list of tools. None of these makes a video worth watching, a
caption worth reading or a subject worth covering. They remove friction and prevent
avoidable mistakes, which is genuinely valuable and is not the same as strategy.

The strategy questions are elsewhere: what to make —
[YouTube keyword research](/blog/youtube-keyword-research) — how often —
[a content calendar that survives a week](/blog/content-calendar-guide) — and which numbers
deserve attention at all — [vanity metrics](/blog/vanity-metrics).

Platform documentation is the other free resource worth using directly: YouTube's
[Creator Academy](https://creatoracademy.youtube.com/) and Meta's
[Business Help Centre](https://www.facebook.com/business/help) both answer mechanical
questions authoritatively, which is more than most third-party guides manage.

:::faq
Q: What free tools do creators actually need?
A: Something to resize images, something to count characters against each platform's real
limits, something to preview a post or link before publishing, and a calculator for
engagement and revenue.
Q: Are free creator tools good enough?
A: For computation over your own inputs, yes. Paid tools earn their keep where data has to
be bought - search volumes, competitor tracking, large-scale analysis.
Q: Do I need an account to use them?
A: Not for the free tier here. Some tools benefit from an account because they save your
history, but the computation itself does not require one.
Q: What is the one tool worth using first?
A: The character counter. Truncation is the most common avoidable mistake in social copy
and it takes ten seconds to check.
:::
