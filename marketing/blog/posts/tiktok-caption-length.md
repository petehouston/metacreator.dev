TikTok caption length has a consequence no other platform's does: the caption sits **on
top of your video**, so every extra line covers more of the frame. The practical limit is
therefore not the character cap but the number of lines you can afford to lose from the
bottom of your composition.

## TikTok caption length has two constraints

| Constraint | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Character cap | Generous — long captions are allowed, including hashtags |
| The fold | Only the first line or two shows before "more" |
| The overlay | Every visible line covers part of your video |

The middle row is familiar from every other platform. The third is what makes TikTok
different, and it is why a caption strategy borrowed from Instagram produces videos with
their subtitles buried.

## The working rule

**One line, occasionally two.** A single line is read, does not cover much, and leaves
your frame intact. Two is acceptable when the second earns its place. Three is a design
decision you probably did not intend to make.

That has a knock-on effect on hashtags: every tag is caption text and consumes the same
space, which is one more reason three to five specific tags beat twenty generic ones —
[TikTok hashtags](/blog/tiktok-hashtags).

## Design around the caption, not despite it

Whatever the caption length, the bottom band of a TikTok frame belongs to the interface:
username, caption, music ticker and progress bar.

[[tool:safe-zone-guide]]

Keep burned-in subtitles in the middle third rather than the lower third — the default
placement in most editing apps is exactly where the caption lands. Detail:
[TikTok video size](/blog/tiktok-video-size) and
[safe zones on social media](/blog/social-media-safe-zones).

## What the caption is for

Three jobs, in order of value on this platform:

1. **Telling TikTok what the video is about.** The recommendation system reads text, and
   a specific caption helps it find the right first audience —
   [TikTok growth](/blog/tiktok-growth-guide).
2. **Adding the thing you could not say on camera.** Context, a source, a correction.
3. **Asking something answerable.** Comments are a strong signal, and they come from
   questions with real answers rather than "thoughts?".

What it is not for is repeating the on-screen text. If the hook is already burned into
the video, the caption should add rather than echo.

TikTok's own guidance on captions and posting is in its
[Creator Portal](https://www.tiktok.com/creators/creator-portal/).

## Compared with other platforms

None of these transfer. [Instagram caption length](/blog/instagram-caption-length) has a
much earlier fold and no overlay problem;
[the Twitter character limit](/blog/twitter-character-limit) is a hard cap with weighted
counting; [where LinkedIn puts See more](/blog/linkedin-see-more) is a fold on a
text-first platform. Writing one caption for all of them produces something that fits
none.

:::faq
Q: How long can a TikTok caption be?
A: Long, including hashtags - but only the first line or two is visible before the fold,
and every visible line covers part of your video.
Q: Do hashtags count toward the TikTok caption length?
A: Yes. They are caption text and take the same space, which is a good reason to use few
and specific ones.
Q: Should my TikTok caption repeat the on-screen text?
A: No. Use it to add context, a source or a question rather than echoing what is already
burned into the video.
Q: Where does the TikTok caption cover the video?
A: The bottom band, above the progress bar. Keep subtitles and important detail in the
middle third of the frame.
:::
