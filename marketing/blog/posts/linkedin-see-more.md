LinkedIn's "see more" fold falls after roughly two to three lines on desktop and fewer on
mobile — usually around 140 to 210 characters, depending on line breaks, word lengths and
screen width. Everything before it decides whether the rest is read at all.

## Why LinkedIn's see more fold has no exact number

The fold is measured in rendered lines, not characters. Three things move it:

- **Line breaks.** A deliberate break ends a line early and consumes part of your budget.
- **Word length.** Long words wrap sooner.
- **Screen width.** A phone shows fewer characters per line than a desktop.

Which is why any guide quoting one precise number is quoting one device.

## Writing the visible part

The two visible lines are a headline, and they should be written like one — the thing
that earns a click, not the setup for it.

Shapes that work:

- **A specific claim.** "We cut our onboarding from 14 days to 3."
- **An admission.** "I got this wrong for two years."
- **A number with context.** "Four out of five applicants skip this step."

Shapes that do not:

- **Throat-clearing.** "I've been thinking a lot recently about…"
- **A greeting.** Nobody expands to read hello.
- **The word "thread" or an emoji ladder.** Both signal formatting rather than substance.

## The deliberate line break

Because the fold counts lines, a single-word line burns a large share of your visible
space. Used deliberately that is a tool — a one-word opening line is arresting — and used
carelessly it wastes the fold.

The habit worth having: write the post, then look at where the fold lands, then adjust the
line breaks rather than the words.

## What not to do with the fold

**Do not engineer a cliffhanger.** Ending the visible portion mid-sentence to force a
click works once and annoys thereafter, and LinkedIn's audience is less tolerant of it
than most.

**Do not put your call to action above the fold.** It is the least interesting sentence
you have, and it is competing for the only space that matters.

**Do not put hashtags at the top.** They consume visible lines and add nothing there —
[LinkedIn hashtags](/blog/linkedin-hashtags).

Every platform truncates somewhere, and none of the positions transfer:

:::faq
Q: How many characters before LinkedIn shows see more?
A: Roughly 140-210, but it is measured in rendered lines rather than characters - so line
breaks and screen width move it.
Q: Does the LinkedIn fold differ on mobile?
A: Yes. Mobile shows fewer characters per line, so the fold falls earlier. Write for the
mobile position.
Q: Does making people click see more help reach?
A: Expanding is a signal LinkedIn measures, but engineering a cliffhanger to force it
annoys readers quickly.
Q: Where should hashtags go in a LinkedIn post?
A: At the end. At the top they consume the only lines that decide whether the post is
read.
:::
