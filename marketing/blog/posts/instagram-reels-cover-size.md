The Instagram Reels cover size is 1080×1920 — 9:16, the same as the Reel itself. The
complication is that the same image is also cropped to a tile in your profile grid, and
the two crops share far less area than people expect. A cover designed for one of them
usually fails in the other.

## Instagram Reels cover size: two crops, one image

| Where | Shape | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reels feed and Reels tab | 9:16 full frame | The whole cover |
| Profile grid | A tall tile, cropped from the centre | Roughly the middle band |

Anything at the very top or bottom of the cover — which is exactly where most people put
their title text — is lost in the grid.

## The rule

**Put the subject and the text in the central band.** Vertically, aim for the middle
half of the frame. If the cover has a title, that title belongs above or below centre —
not at the very top, and never at the very bottom where the caption and interface also
sit.

The interface bands matter for the full-frame view too: username, caption and the action
rail cover the edges of a playing Reel —
[safe zones](/blog/social-media-safe-zones) and
[Instagram Story size](/blog/instagram-story-size) cover the same geometry.

## Choosing the cover frame

Instagram lets you pick a frame from the video or upload an image. Upload one, almost
always — a frame grabbed from the video is a frame of somebody mid-blink at whatever
resolution the encode produced.

What makes a cover work in a grid of other covers:

- **A face or a single clear subject.** Grids are scanned, not read.
- **Three or four words of text**, large. At tile size, a sentence is texture.
- **Consistent treatment across your Reels.** Recognition is what makes a profile look
  like a body of work rather than a pile of posts.

## Getting the export right

Export the cover at 1080×1920 as a JPEG or PNG, then check the grid crop before
publishing rather than after. Instagram recompresses, so an oversized file is downscaled
by their encoder rather than yours —

## Why a good cover matters more than it looks

A Reels cover does two jobs that have nothing to do with each other, which is why it is
easy to do one and forget the other.

In the **Reels feed**, the cover barely matters - the video autoplays and the first
frame of motion is what holds someone. In the **profile grid**, the cover is everything:
a visitor deciding whether to follow you is looking at nine tiles, and those tiles are
the entire impression your account makes.

So the cover is not really a thumbnail. It is a component of your profile, and it should
be designed as part of a set rather than one at a time. Accounts whose grids look
deliberate are almost always using a consistent cover treatment - the same typeface, the
same position, the same colour treatment - and it takes no more effort than doing it
differently every time.

The test: open your own profile and squint. If the tiles read as a collection, the
covers are working. If they read as a pile, no individual cover will fix it.

:::faq
Q: What size is an Instagram Reels cover?
A: 1080×1920 at 9:16, the same as the Reel. It is also cropped to a tile in your profile
grid, so the important content must sit near the centre.
Q: Can I change a Reels cover after posting?
A: Instagram's editing options for a published Reel are limited and have changed over
time. Treat the cover as something to get right before publishing.
Q: Why does my Reels cover look cropped in my profile?
A: The grid shows a tile cropped from the centre of the 9:16 cover. Text at the top or
bottom of the frame is cut.
Q: Should I upload a cover or pick a frame?
A: Upload one. A frame from the video is rarely composed, and it is at whatever quality
the encode produced.
:::
