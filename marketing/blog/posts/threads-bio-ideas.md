Threads bio ideas need to solve a slightly different problem from bios elsewhere: most
people who look at your Threads profile arrive from a single reply you left under someone
else's post. They have seen one sentence of yours, out of context, and are deciding
whether that was a fluke.

## The template

```text
[What you do, in a noun phrase.]
[The thing you post about, specifically.]
[One link or one location, if either matters.]
```

Worked:

```text
Coffee equipment teardowns.
I take the machine apart so you don't have to.
```

Short is correct here. The Threads bio field is small, the profile is usually seen on a
phone, and a visitor arriving from a reply has already given you more attention than
average.

## What works, given how people arrive

**Say what you post about, not who you are.** The visitor is deciding whether to expect
more of what they just read. "Coffee equipment teardowns" answers that; "curious human,
coffee lover, dad" does not.

**Match the register of your replies.** A profile that reads as corporate under replies
that read as casual produces a mismatch that costs you the follow.

**One link, at most.** Threads gives you a link field. Tag it if you publish it in more
than one place so you know which profile sent the click:

## Threads bio ideas by account type

**Writer or commentator**
```text
I write about [subject].
Usually here, occasionally at [publication].
```

**Practitioner**
```text
[Discipline], [number] years.
Posting the parts nobody writes down.
```

**Business or product**
```text
[What it does] for [who].
Support: [handle] · [link]
```

## The handle matters more here than elsewhere

Because so much of your visibility is in replies, your handle and display name are read
constantly, at small size, in other people's threads. A handle that is hard to read is a
handle people do not remember.

## What to leave out

- **Emoji standing in for nouns.** Unreadable to screen readers, ambiguous to everyone.
- **A list of unrelated interests.** It answers a question nobody asked.
- **"Opinions my own."** Nobody has ever been reassured by this.
- **Hashtags.** They send a visitor away from the page where they were deciding to
  follow you.

Styled Unicode text has the same accessibility cost here as on Instagram — see
[Instagram fonts](/blog/instagram-fonts) for why it should stay decorative rather than
carry meaning.

:::faq
Q: What should a Threads bio say?
A: What you post about, in a specific noun phrase, and at most one link. Visitors usually
arrive from one reply and want to know whether there is more of it.
Q: How long can a Threads bio be?
A: Short. The field is small and the profile is read on a phone, so a two-line bio is the
practical target.
Q: Should my Threads bio match my Instagram one?
A: The voice should match; the text does not have to. Threads visitors arrive from
replies, Instagram visitors from posts, and those are different moments.
Q: Do hashtags in a Threads bio help?
A: No. They give a visitor somewhere else to go at exactly the wrong moment.
:::
