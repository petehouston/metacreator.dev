A fake social media post generator draws a platform's post card from text you type, and
exports it as an image. It is a **mock-up tool, not an evidence tool**: the card proves
nothing about who posted what, because nothing was posted. That single distinction
decides every legitimate and illegitimate use of one.

## What people actually use a fake social media post generator for

Four uses cover almost all of it, and none of them involve deceiving anybody.

- **Slides and articles about something that was posted.** Screenshotting the real
  thing drags in a sidebar, a cookie banner, a notification and whoever else was in the
  feed. A drawn card is the post and nothing else.
- **Mock-ups of copy that has not been published yet.** Showing a client or a team what
  a caption will look like in place, before it is written into the scheduler.
- **Teaching examples.** A real post dates instantly — the account changes its name, the
  post is deleted, the platform redesigns the card. A drawn one does not.
- **Checking where the text is cut.** Instagram hides a caption past roughly 125
  characters; Pinterest cuts a Pin title around 40. Seeing the sentence that disappears
  is the whole point, and it is why these tools overlap with the preview family — see
  [Instagram caption length](/blog/instagram-caption-length).

## Where the line is

Passing off a drawn card as a screenshot of a real post is impersonation, defamation, or
both, depending on where you are and whose name is on it. A disclaimer in a caption does
not undo it, because the image travels on its own: the screenshot gets reposted, the
caption does not.

The specific things that turn a mock-up into a forgery:

1. **A real person's or brand's name and handle** on words they did not write.
2. **A verified badge**, which is the single element that makes a card claim
   authenticity.
3. **A real photograph composited in**, which removes the last visual cue that the image
   was made rather than captured.
4. **Presenting it without context** in a thread, a video, or a news story.

The first is a decision only you can make. The other three are decisions a tool can make
for you, which is what the next section is about.

## Three rules these generators follow

Every card on this site is drawn to the same three constraints, and they are not
configurable.

**No verification badge, on any card, ever.** There is no legitimate use for a fake blue
tick that outweighs the obvious illegitimate ones, so the option does not exist.

**No real photograph in the media slot.** The Instagram and Pinterest cards draw a
marked placeholder at the platform's own aspect ratio. Compositing an image into a
real-looking post would make these a forgery kit rather than a mock-up tool — and the
placeholder still answers the question people actually have, which is where the caption
falls. Drop your own image in behind the card in whatever you are building.

**A warning on every export.** Once in the result, once under the canvas.

There is one deliberate exception: an **avatar** can be added, because a mock-up of your
own account is a normal thing to want. It is read in your browser and drawn straight
onto the canvas, so nothing is uploaded and there is no copy of it on our side.

:::warning
If the card carries a real person's handle, and the words are not theirs, the tool it
was drawn with is not the thing that matters. Do not do it.
:::

## A drawn card versus a real screenshot

If what you need is a clean image of a post that genuinely exists, a mock-up is the
wrong tool — you would be retyping something you already have, and introducing an
opportunity to get it wrong.

The better options, in order: **embed the post**, which keeps it under its author's
control and disappears if they delete it ([how to embed a social media
post](/blog/embed-a-social-media-post)); screenshot it and crop hard; or, for X
specifically, use the [post screenshot generator](/tools/tweet-screenshot-generator),
which draws a clean card at any size.

For the legal background on using other people's posts, the Digital Media Law Project's
[guidance on using the work of
others](https://www.dmlp.org/legal-guide/using-work-others) is a reasonable starting
point, though it is US-centric.

:::faq
Q: Is it legal to make a fake social media post?
A: Making a mock-up is. Publishing one as though a real person or business said something they did not is a different matter — defamation and impersonation laws apply regardless of how the image was produced.
Q: Why will these tools not draw a verified badge?
A: Because a badge is what makes a card claim to be authentic. There is no legitimate use for a fake one that outweighs the obvious misuse, so it is not an option.
Q: Why can I not add the actual photo to an Instagram mock-up?
A: By design. A generator that composited a real image into a real-looking post would be a forgery kit rather than a mock-up tool. The placeholder sits at Instagram's own aspect ratio, so your image lines up exactly if you drop it in behind the card.
Q: Are the images I make uploaded anywhere?
A: No. Every card is drawn on a canvas in your own browser, including any avatar you add. There is no request to inspect and nothing stored on our side.
:::
