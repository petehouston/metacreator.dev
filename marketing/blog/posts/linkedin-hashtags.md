Use about three LinkedIn hashtags, at the end of the post. LinkedIn publishes no hard
limit, but three is where professional convention settled — and on a network where
posts are judged partly on whether they look considered, a wall of tags is a signal in
itself.

## How many LinkedIn hashtags to use

| Number | How it reads |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Fine. Many high-performing posts use none |
| 1-3 | The convention. Looks deliberate |
| 4-6 | Tolerable, starts to look like marketing |
| 7+ | Reads as automated or desperate |

There is no penalty documented for using more. The cost is reputational, and on
LinkedIn that is the cost that matters — the audience is professional peers, and the
post is partly a judgement about you.

## What LinkedIn hashtags actually do

They categorise the post and make it findable by people following that topic. LinkedIn
lets members follow hashtags, so a well-chosen tag can put a post in front of people who
are not connected to you — which is genuinely the main mechanism by which a LinkedIn
post travels beyond your own network.

What they do not do is boost reach in proportion to their number. Three relevant tags
put you in three topic feeds; twenty irrelevant tags put you in none that matter.

## Choosing three

- **One broad industry tag.** The field you are in.
- **One specific topic tag.** What this post is actually about.
- **One community or format tag**, if a genuine one exists in your field.

Prefer tags people actually follow over ones you invent. A tag with no followers is a
label, not a distribution channel — and unlike Instagram, LinkedIn shows you the
follower count of a hashtag when you type it, which is unusually helpful.

## Where they go, and why the fold matters

At the end of the post. LinkedIn truncates the text behind a "see more" link after a
few lines, and hashtags placed early consume the space that decides whether anyone
expands it.

## Following hashtags is the underused half

The feature most people ignore: you can follow a hashtag yourself, and doing so changes
what LinkedIn shows you. For anyone using the platform to find work, clients or peers
rather than to broadcast, that is more valuable than any tagging strategy.

Follow the three or four tags your field actually uses, and the feed becomes a stream of
what people in that field are discussing rather than whatever your first-degree
connections happened to like. It also tells you, for free, which tags are alive - a tag
whose feed is full of recruiters and automated posts is a tag not worth putting on your
own writing.

That is the honest test for any tag you are considering: open its feed and read the last
twenty posts. If they are good, the tag is a real community. If they are spam, adding it
puts your post in a spam feed.

## What matters more than the tags

On LinkedIn, engagement in the first hour drives distribution more than any topical
signal. Two practical consequences:

**The first two lines are the whole post** as far as reach is concerned, because they
are what people see before deciding to expand. Everything about structuring that is in
[LinkedIn post length, formats and reach](/blog/linkedin-post-guide).

**Format outranks tags.** A document post occupies more feed space and holds attention
longer than a text post with perfect tagging —
[LinkedIn carousel](/blog/linkedin-carousel).

LinkedIn's own guidance for creators is in its
[help centre](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin), and the cross-network comparison
— because none of this transfers to Instagram or YouTube — is in
[how to use hashtags](/blog/how-to-use-hashtags).

:::faq
Q: How many hashtags should I use on LinkedIn?
A: About three. There is no documented limit, but more than a handful reads as
automated on a professional network.
Q: Do LinkedIn hashtags increase reach?
A: Modestly. Members can follow hashtags, so a relevant tag can reach people outside
your network - but engagement in the first hour matters far more.
Q: Where should hashtags go in a LinkedIn post?
A: At the end. Placing them early wastes the lines that decide whether anyone expands
the post.
Q: Should I use hashtags in LinkedIn comments?
A: There is no benefit. Unlike Instagram, the convention is simply to put them at the
end of the post itself.
:::
