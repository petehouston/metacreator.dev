How much to charge for an Instagram post comes down to three things: how many people
actually see it, what a thousand of them are worth in your niche, and how engaged they
are. Followers are a proxy for the first of those and a poor one — which is why the
per-follower rule underprices good accounts and overprices big quiet ones.

## Instagram pays you nothing for the post

Start here, because it clears away half the confusion.

There is no ad-revenue share on Instagram. No RPM, no payout per view, no equivalent of
the YouTube Partner Programme. Bonus programmes have opened and closed in various
countries and none of them are a business. **The money on Instagram is brand deals**, so
the honest version of this question is "what does my rate card say", and a rate card is
built from reach.

That makes Instagram simpler to price than YouTube, where the platform's own payout
muddies the picture — compare [CPM vs RPM](/blog/cpm-vs-rpm), which is the equivalent
arithmetic when the platform is paying you directly.

## How to charge for an Instagram post: the formula

```text
rate = (reach ÷ 1000) × niche CPM × format multiplier × engagement band
```

**Reach**, not followers. Use your own average reach per post from Insights — it is the
number to quote in a media kit and it is frequently better than a follower count would
suggest, especially on Reels.

**Niche CPM** is what a brand pays per thousand people reached, and it varies
enormously: finance and B2B software at the top, entertainment and meme accounts at the
bottom. The same 20,000 reach is worth three times more in one niche than another.

Whatever the rate, the deal has to be disclosed. Meta requires the paid-partnership
label and sets out what counts in its
[branded content policies](https://www.facebook.com/policies/brandedcontent/), and
advertising regulators in most countries require a clear disclosure in the caption on
top of it. Neither is negotiable, and neither belongs in the small print of your
quote.

**Format multiplier.** A Reel is served beyond your follower graph and prices above a
feed post. A Story frame is cheap and gone in a day.

**Engagement band** is the multiplier that separates two accounts of the same size, and
it is applied to the CPM rather than bolted on afterwards. A 25,000-follower account at
6% is worth more per post than a 100,000-follower account at 0.8%, and any brand with a
media buyer knows it. Work your rate out with the
[engagement rate calculator](/tools/engagement-rate-calculator) before you price
anything.

## Quote three numbers, not one

A single figure invites the buyer to treat it as a ceiling and negotiate down from there.
So the [Instagram money calculator](/tools/instagram-money-calculator) returns three, and
you should think in three:

**The ask.** What you open with. Brands expect to negotiate and the first number sets
the range.

**The fair rate.** Where a reasonable deal lands. This is your real price.

**The floor.** Below this you are paying for the privilege of making the content —
shooting, editing, revisions, and the post sitting on your grid permanently. Say no
below it, politely, and say why.

## The clause creators give away for free

Usage rights.

"We may also run this as an ad for six months" is not a formality. It is a **media buy**,
and it is frequently worth more than the post itself: the brand gets your face, your
words and your credibility in a paid placement, at whatever budget they choose, to
whoever they choose.

Price it separately. A common structure is the post fee plus a percentage for a defined
period and a defined set of placements. If the brand will not define the period, the
answer is not a lower percentage; it is a defined period.

The same applies to **exclusivity** — a clause preventing you working with competitors
for three months is a real cost to your business and belongs in the number.

## Build the rate card

1. Open Insights and take your **average reach** for the format you are selling.
2. Take your **engagement rate** for the same period.
3. Put both into the calculator with your niche and format.
4. Write down the three numbers, and do not send the floor to anybody.
5. Add lines for usage rights, exclusivity and revision limits before you quote.

Two extras worth having ready: a fixed number of revisions (two is normal, unlimited is
how a $600 post becomes a week of work), and who has approval over the caption. On which
— the caption is where the disclosure goes, and it is also where a fold will cut your
call to action if you are not watching. The
[social media character counter](/tools/social-media-character-counter) shows where each
surface truncates.

## What to do when they ask for your rate first

The buyer almost always asks first, and answering with a question reads as evasive. So
answer with the ask, and attach the two things that make it defensible:

**The reach figure it is based on.** "My last ten Reels averaged 38,000 reach" is a fact
they can price against. A follower count is not.

**What the fee covers.** One Reel, one revision round, thirty days of organic life, no
paid usage. Stating the boundary at the same time as the number stops the scope from
growing quietly afterwards.

If the budget comes back below your floor, the useful counter is not a discount — it is a
smaller deliverable at the same rate. Three Story frames instead of a Reel. A feed post
instead of a Reel. That keeps your rate intact for the next negotiation, which is the
thing a discount quietly destroys.

## Where the bands come from

The rates in the calculator are the ranges sponsorship marketplaces and agency rate
cards cluster around. They are not a measurement of any one deal, and they are not a
quote. What they are for is putting a negotiation in the right order of magnitude, which
is the thing a creator asking this question is usually missing entirely.

Real deals move on things no calculator sees: audience country, how well the brand
already knows you, whether it is one post or six, and whether the brief is a product shot
or a script somebody else wrote.

:::faq
Q: How much should I charge per 1,000 followers on Instagram?
A: That figure is the one people compare and the one that hides everything that matters. Price on reach times a niche rate times your engagement band instead; the per-follower number is an output, not an input.
Q: How much does a 10k Instagram account charge?
A: Anywhere from about $100 to several hundred dollars per post depending on niche, format and engagement rate. The spread between two 10k accounts is larger than the spread between a 10k and a 50k in the same niche.
Q: Should I charge for Stories separately?
A: Sell them in sets or add them to a Reel deal. A single Story frame reaches a fraction of your followers and is gone in a day, so pricing one alone rarely covers the work.
Q: What should I charge for usage rights?
A: A percentage of the post fee for a defined period and defined placements. If the brand will not define the period, do not lower the percentage — insist on the definition.
:::
