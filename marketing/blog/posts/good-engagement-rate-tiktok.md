A good TikTok engagement rate is roughly 4-8% calculated against followers, which is
much higher than the equivalent Instagram figure — and that difference is mostly
structural rather than a sign that TikTok audiences are more enthusiastic.

## A good TikTok engagement rate, by size

| Followers | Weak | Typical | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Under 10,000 | Under 4% | 6-12% | Above 12% |
| 10,000-100,000 | Under 3% | 5-9% | Above 9% |
| 100,000-1M | Under 2.5% | 4-7% | Above 7% |
| Over 1M | Under 2% | 3-5% | Above 5% |

Calculated as (likes + comments + shares) ÷ followers × 100. As with every platform,
these are directional bands rather than published figures — TikTok does not release
benchmark data, and every study measuring it uses a different sample.

## Why TikTok's numbers run higher

Three reasons, and none of them is that TikTok users care more:

1. **Distribution is not follower-bound.** A TikTok is shown to people who do not
   follow you, so interactions can exceed what your follower count would predict. On a
   follower-based formula that inflates the ratio.
2. **Shares are visible and countable.** On most platforms shares are private to the
   account owner, so external calculations exclude them. On TikTok they are on the
   video.
3. **The interaction is cheap.** A double-tap during a full-screen video costs nothing.

The consequence is that a TikTok engagement rate and an Instagram engagement rate are
not comparable numbers, even when both are calculated the same way. Anyone presenting
them side by side in a media kit should label them clearly.

## The formula that describes reality on TikTok

**Engagement by views** — (likes + comments + shares) ÷ views × 100 — is the honest
measure on a platform where views are public and reach is not follower-bound. It
produces a much smaller number, typically low single digits, and it answers the question
that matters: of the people who saw this, how many did something.

Use views-based internally to judge videos against each other. Use followers-based
externally, because that is what a brand will calculate. Say which you used, every time.
The reasoning is in
[engagement rate by reach vs by followers](/blog/engagement-rate-by-reach-vs-followers)
and the formulas in [engagement rate](/blog/engagement-rate).

## What actually moves it

**Completion rate**, which is not engagement but drives it — a video people finish gets
shown to more people, and more people means more interactions.

**Comment bait that is not bait.** A genuine question in the caption or the last frame
earns comments; "comment below!" does not.

**Shares.** The most valuable interaction on the platform, because a share is a person
spending their own reputation on your video.

**Hashtags**, barely. They are a topic signal that helps the first audience be
well-matched, which matters — but they are not a lever you pull for engagement:
[TikTok hashtags](/blog/tiktok-hashtags).

The distribution mechanics behind all of this are in
[TikTok growth](/blog/tiktok-growth-guide), and TikTok's own account of how the For You
feed works is in its
[Creator Portal](https://www.tiktok.com/creators/creator-portal/).

## When the number is misleading

**A single viral video wrecks the average.** One video with ten million views and a
normal number of interactions drags a views-based rate down and a followers-based rate
up. Use a median across recent videos.

**A dormant follower count.** Followers accumulated during a viral moment often never
watch again. This lowers followers-based engagement without saying anything about
current performance.

**Follower count is a weak denominator here anyway**, because so much reach is
non-follower. That is the strongest argument for tracking views-based engagement as your
own internal number.

:::faq
Q: What is a good engagement rate on TikTok?
A: Roughly 4-8% against followers for most accounts, higher for smaller ones. Against
views it is a much smaller number, usually low single digits.
Q: Why is my TikTok engagement rate higher than my Instagram one?
A: Mostly structure: TikTok distributes beyond your followers and counts shares
publicly, both of which inflate a follower-based ratio.
Q: Should I use views or followers for TikTok engagement?
A: Views internally, because it describes what actually happened. Followers externally,
because that is what a brand can verify.
Q: Does a viral video improve my engagement rate?
A: It usually lowers the views-based rate and raises the followers-based one. Use a
median across recent videos rather than an average.
:::
