The Instagram giveaway rules that matter are the ones you publish, not the ones Instagram
enforces. A giveaway is a trade: you give away something of value and you get attention,
entries and — if you design it badly — a large number of followers who wanted the prize
and nothing else.

## Instagram giveaway rules to publish, every time

Instagram's promotion guidelines require the organiser to run the promotion lawfully,
publish official rules and eligibility requirements, and acknowledge that the promotion
is not sponsored or endorsed by Instagram
([Instagram Help](https://help.instagram.com/179379842258600)). In practice that means a
short block of text on the post itself or a link to it:

```text
Prize: [exactly what, and how many]
Entry: [exactly what someone must do]
Who can enter: [countries, minimum age]
Closes: [date and time, with timezone]
Winner: drawn at random from valid entries, announced [where]
Not sponsored by, administered by or associated with Instagram.
```

The specifics vary by jurisdiction, and a prize of real value in a regulated market is
worth a lawyer's five minutes. What is universal is that publishing rules before entries
open prevents every argument that follows.

## Entry mechanics, ranked

| Mechanic | Attracts | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Comment an answer to a question | People interested in the subject | Best |
| Save the post | Your existing audience | Good, unverifiable |
| Tag a friend | Reach, and some noise | Acceptable in moderation |
| Follow to enter | Prize-hunters | Use sparingly |
| Follow five accounts, tag ten friends | Bots and loop-giveaway accounts | Avoid entirely |

The trade-off is direct: the harder the entry is to fake, and the more it relates to your
subject, the smaller and better the entrant pool.

**Choose the prize to filter, not to attract.** An iPad brings everyone. A thing only
useful to people in your niche brings the audience you actually want, and it usually
costs less.

## Loop giveaways, and why they end badly

The mechanic where entrants must follow a list of accounts - usually assembled by several
creators pooling a prize - reliably produces the worst outcome available.

Everyone involved gains followers on the day. Within a fortnight most of them unfollow,
and the ones who stay were interested in a prize rather than in any of the accounts. The
result is a permanently lower engagement rate for everyone in the loop, and an audience
that has learned that this account gives things away rather than that it is worth
following.

Two related mechanics that fail the same way:

**Comment-tag chains.** "Tag three friends, one per comment" inflates comment counts with
strings of usernames. Brands increasingly read comments rather than count them, and a
comment section of tagged names is visibly worthless.

**Follow-to-enter with a broad prize.** The prize determines the audience. A generic
high-value item guarantees an audience with no common interest except free things.

If a giveaway is meant to reward the people who already follow you - which is the one
version that reliably works - the entry requirement should be something only they can
sensibly do.

## After the giveaway

Expect a follower drop. People who followed for a prize unfollow when it is over, and
that is the system working as intended — you would rather they left than sat inert in
your denominator lowering your engagement rate.

The measurement worth doing is not follower count but engagement rate a month later,
compared with a month before. If it fell and stayed down, the giveaway bought you an

:::faq
Q: Are giveaways allowed on Instagram?
A: Yes, with conditions - you must publish official rules and eligibility, comply with
the law where you operate, and state that Instagram is not involved.
Q: Do Instagram giveaways grow your account?
A: They grow follower count. Whether they grow an audience depends entirely on the prize
and the entry mechanic.
Q: What is the best entry mechanic?
A: Answering a question in a comment. It is hard to automate and it attracts people who
care about the subject.
Q: How do I pick a winner fairly?
A: Draw at random from valid entries using a method anyone can reproduce - a published
seed makes the result verifiable rather than merely asserted.
