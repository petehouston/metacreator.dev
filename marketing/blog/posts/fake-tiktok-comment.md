## Three details that give a fake TikTok comment away

Almost every generator gets at least one of these wrong, and each of them is noticeable
to anybody who uses the app daily.

**The Creator chip.** TikTok marks the video's own author with a small grey chip reading
"Creator", placed **after the handle**. It is not a heart, and it is not in the action
row — that is YouTube's convention, and borrowing it is the fastest tell there is.

**The age format.** TikTok writes `3h`, never "3 hours ago". A spelled-out relative time
under a TikTok comment is wrong at a glance even though almost nobody could tell you the
rule.

**The heart column.** The like button sits at the **right edge** of the card with its
count underneath it, not inline with "Reply". A comment card with a heart in the action
row is a YouTube card wearing TikTok's colours.

## Dark or light

Dark, almost always. The TikTok app is dark by default and that is what people
recognise; a light TikTok comment reads as unfamiliar even to somebody who has never
consciously noticed the theme.

The light version matches the web comment panel, and it is the one to use when the card
has to sit on a white page — a blog post, a printed deck, a document — where a black
rectangle would fight the layout.

## The counts, and making them plausible

Two things to get right.

TikTok abbreviates counts as `1K`, `5.2K`, `1.4M`, with one decimal place and no
trailing zero — it writes `5K`, never `5.0K`. Generators that print a raw `5000` are
immediately wrong.

And the ratio matters more than the numbers. A comment with 12,400 likes and no replies
is odd; one with more replies than likes is odder. If you are mocking up for a
presentation, take the shape from a real comment you have seen rather than picking round
numbers.

You can also mark the comment as **pinned**, which draws the small label TikTok puts
above a pinned comment, and as **liked by creator**, which adds the line underneath.
Both are common in the comment-as-hook format, because both are what make a comment
worth screenshotting in the first place.

TikTok's own documentation of the comment interface is in its
[Help Centre](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/messaging-and-notifications/comments).

## What the card is and is not

It draws whatever you type. It is a mock-up, not proof, and it carries no verified
badge on purpose.

The risk here is sharper than for a static post mock-up, and it is worth naming plainly:
a comment card used as the hook on a video reaches a great deal further than any
correction ever will. Putting a real person's handle on words they did not write is not
a grey area, and "it was obviously a joke" is a defence that stops working the moment
the clip is reposted without your caption.
