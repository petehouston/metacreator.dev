The Facebook ad character limit that matters is not the field maximum — it is the point
at which each field is truncated in the feed. Meta's own recommendations are far shorter
than what the fields accept: around 125 characters of primary text, about 40 for the
headline and about 30 for the description
([Meta Business Help](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/103816146375741)).

## Facebook ad character limits, by field

| Field | Recommended | What happens past it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary text | ~125 characters | Truncated behind "See more" |
| Headline | ~40 characters | Truncated, often mid-word |
| Description | ~30 characters | Frequently not shown at all on mobile |

The description row is the one that surprises people: on several placements it is not
displayed, so anything essential placed there is invisible to most of the audience.

## Writing to the truncation, not the limit

**Primary text.** The first sentence carries the offer. Assume the rest is read only by
people already interested — which is a reasonable assumption and a useful discipline.

**Headline.** This is the strongest element on most placements after the image. Forty
characters is a short phrase, so it should be the benefit rather than the brand.

**Description.** Treat it as optional reinforcement. Never put the offer, the price or the
deadline there alone.

## The image is doing most of the work anyway

Ad copy competes with an image that is read first and faster. Getting the image right
matters more than any of the character counts above:

Sizes are in [Facebook image sizes](/blog/facebook-image-sizes). Meta no longer enforces
a hard limit on text within an image, but the underlying behaviour has not changed —
text-heavy images perform worse in a feed, and a paragraph inside an image is unreadable,
untranslatable and inaccessible.

## Why the recommendations are so much shorter than the fields

The gap between what a field accepts and what Meta recommends is not a mistake. The
fields are generous because placements differ - an ad on a desktop feed has more room
than one in a Story or a Reels overlay, and the same creative is served to all of them.

The recommendation describes the tightest common placement. Write to it and your ad reads
correctly everywhere; write to the field maximum and it reads correctly in one placement
and is cut in the rest.

There is a second reason to stay short that has nothing to do with truncation. Feed
advertising competes for a fraction of a second against organic content the person
actually chose to see. A long ad is not read and then rejected - it is not read at all,
which is why the strongest-performing ad copy tends to be dramatically shorter than the
limits allow.

## Organic posts have different constraints

Nothing here transfers to an organic Facebook post, which has no equivalent structure and

## Before you launch

Check what the ad actually looks like, rather than what the form shows. The two most
common launch-day discoveries are a headline cut mid-word and a link card pulling the
wrong image from the destination page — the second of which is fixed on the page, not in
the ad:

:::faq
Q: What is the Facebook ad character limit for primary text?
A: The field accepts more, but Meta recommends around 125 characters because the feed
truncates past roughly that point.
Q: How long should a Facebook ad headline be?
A: About 40 characters. It is truncated beyond that, often mid-word, and it is the
strongest text element on most placements.
Q: Is the description field shown on mobile?
A: Frequently not. Do not put anything essential there on its own.
Q: Does Facebook still limit text in ad images?
A: Not as a hard rule. Text-heavy images still underperform, so the old 20% guidance
remains sound advice even though it is no longer enforced.
:::
