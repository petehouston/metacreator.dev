A good Instagram engagement rate is roughly 1-3% for most accounts calculated against
followers, and higher for smaller ones. The single most important thing to know before
comparing yourself to any published figure is which formula it used — reach-based
figures are several times larger than follower-based ones for the same post.

## A good Instagram engagement rate, by follower count

| Followers | Weak | Typical | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Under 5,000 | Under 2% | 3-6% | Above 6% |
| 5,000-25,000 | Under 1.5% | 2-4% | Above 4% |
| 25,000-100,000 | Under 1% | 1.5-3% | Above 3% |
| Over 100,000 | Under 0.8% | 1-2% | Above 2% |

Calculated as (likes + comments) ÷ followers × 100. These are directional bands drawn
from what accounts commonly report, not published figures — Instagram does not release
benchmark data, and every agency study sampling it produces slightly different numbers
from a different sample.

## Why smaller accounts always score higher

Not because they are better. Three structural reasons:

1. **A small following is self-selected.** People who found you deliberately engage
   more than people who followed a viral video once.
2. **Reach is proportionally higher.** A new account's posts are shown to a larger
   share of its followers than a large account's are.
3. **The denominator grows faster than attention.** Followers accumulate; the number of
   people actively watching does not grow at the same rate.

The practical consequence: **compare yourself to accounts of your own size**, and treat
a falling engagement rate during a period of follower growth as normal rather than as a
problem.

## Which formula Instagram's own numbers support

If you have access to Insights, engagement by reach is the honest measure of whether a
post worked — it tells you what share of the people who actually saw it responded.
Engagement by followers is the right number to quote externally, because a brand can
verify it without your analytics.

The distinction is worked through in
[engagement rate by reach vs by followers](/blog/engagement-rate-by-reach-vs-followers),

## What moves the number

**Format.** Carousels and Reels typically out-engage single images, partly because they
have more surfaces to interact with and partly because they are distributed more
widely — see [Instagram carousel size](/blog/instagram-carousel-size).

**Saves and shares.** Weighted heavily by Instagram and usually excluded from
externally calculated engagement rates, which means your internal number and a brand's
estimate of it can differ substantially.

**Caption quality.** Comments are the expensive interaction, and they mostly come from
captions that ask something answerable —
[Instagram caption length](/blog/instagram-caption-length).

**Consistency.** An account posting weekly has a warmer audience than one posting in
bursts.

## How to compare yourself to a competitor honestly

You cannot see anyone else's reach, so any comparison with another account has to use
the follower-based formula on visible interactions only - likes and comments. That is
fine, as long as you apply the same restriction to yourself.

The method that produces a fair number:

1. Take their last twelve posts, excluding anything obviously exceptional.
2. Add likes and comments for each, divide by their follower count, times 100.
3. Take the median, not the mean.
4. Do exactly the same to your own account, ignoring your saves and reach data.

The result is comparable. The temptation to skip step four - to compare your
reach-based internal figure against their follower-based external one - is where most
competitive analysis goes wrong, and it always flatters you.

## When a low engagement rate means something is wrong

A number well below the band for your size, with no other explanation, usually points at
one of:

- **Purchased or incentivised followers** — the denominator is inflated with accounts
  that will never engage.
- **A giveaway audience** — people who followed for a prize and stayed inert. If you
  run giveaways, run them so they attract the right people:
  [Instagram giveaway rules](/blog/instagram-giveaway-rules).
- **A shift in subject** that lost the original audience without gaining a new one.

Instagram's own explanation of how its ranking works is in its
[creator resources](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained),
and it is a better guide than most third-party speculation.

:::faq
Q: What is a good engagement rate on Instagram?
A: Around 1-3% against followers for mid-size accounts, and 3-6% or more for accounts
under 5,000 followers.
Q: Why is my engagement rate dropping as I gain followers?
A: Because followers grow faster than active attention. A gently falling rate during
growth is normal, not a warning.
Q: Should I include saves and shares?
A: Include them for your own analysis and say so. Exclude them when quoting a figure
externally, because a brand cannot see them.
Q: Is 1% a bad engagement rate on Instagram?
A: For an account over 100,000 followers, no - it is normal. For an account under
5,000, it suggests an audience that is not really yours.
:::
