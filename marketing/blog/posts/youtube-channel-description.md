A YouTube channel description has one job above the fold: tell a stranger what they
get if they subscribe. Roughly the first 150 characters carry that job — they appear
on the channel page before the reader expands anything, and they are what shows in
search results and previews.

## The structure that works

Three blocks, in this order:

1. **What the channel is, in one sentence.** Subject, format, and who it is for.
   "Weekly deep-dives on home espresso, for people who already own a machine."
2. **What makes it different, in one sentence.** Not adjectives. A method, a
   credential, a constraint. "Every recommendation tested for at least a month."
3. **The schedule.** "New videos every Tuesday." This one line converts browsers into
   subscribers more reliably than any amount of enthusiasm.

Everything after that — links, contact, credits, affiliate disclosure, keyword lists —
is below the fold and read by almost nobody. Which is fine. It just must not be first.

## Why the first line is not the place for keywords

Channel descriptions are frequently written as keyword lists, on the theory that
YouTube reads them. YouTube may well read them; the human deciding whether to
subscribe definitely does, and a list of comma-separated phrases tells that person
nothing except that you are optimising at them.

The searchable words belong in the sentence, naturally, where they do both jobs at
once. That is the same principle that governs
[YouTube title length](/blog/youtube-title-length): the words that get you found and
the words that get you chosen should be the same words.

## Channel description versus video description

Different fields, different jobs, and pasting one into the other is a common mistake:

| | Channel description | Video description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Answers | Why subscribe to this channel | What is in this video |
| Fold | ~150 characters | ~2-3 lines |
| Rewritten | Once or twice a year | Every upload |
| Contains | Positioning, schedule, links | Promise, chapters, links |

## The rest of the channel page

The About text sits alongside several other things a visitor judges you by, and they
are worth fixing in the same sitting:

- **Banner.** 2048×1152 minimum, with a safe area of 1235×338 for anything that must
  be visible on every device
  ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12950272)). A tagline in
  the corner is a tagline nobody sees on a television.
- **Handle.** Short, sayable, consistent with your other networks — see
  [YouTube handles](/blog/youtube-handles).
- **Featured video.** A trailer for new visitors is a genuinely different video from
  your latest upload, and channels that make one convert better.
- **Links.** Fewer than you think. Five links means none of them are clicked.

## Testing whether it works

Read the first 150 characters out loud to somebody who does not know your channel and
ask what they think it is about. If they hesitate, the sentence is about you rather
than about them — the most common failure in this field is a description that
describes the creator's journey instead of the viewer's benefit.

:::faq
Q: How long should a YouTube channel description be?
A: The visible part is roughly 150 characters, so put the positioning there. The full
field can be longer for links and credits, but almost nobody reads past the fold.
Q: Do YouTube channel descriptions affect search?
A: They give YouTube text about the channel and they appear in previews. They will not
rank a channel on their own, and keyword lists cost you the reader.
Q: What should a YouTube channel description include?
A: What the channel is, what makes it different, and when new videos appear. Links and
contact details go below the fold.
Q: Is the channel description the same as the video description?
A: No. One explains why to subscribe, the other explains what is in a single video.
Copying between them wastes both.
:::
