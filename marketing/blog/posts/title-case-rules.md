Title case capitalises the significant words in a heading; sentence case capitalises only
the first word and proper nouns. Neither is a ranking factor. What matters is picking one
and applying it everywhere, because inconsistency is the thing readers notice.

## Title case and sentence case, defined

**Title Case Looks Like This.** Major words capitalised, small ones not.

**Sentence case looks like this.** First word, proper nouns, nothing else.

The complication is that "major words" is defined differently by every style guide. AP
capitalises words of four letters or more; Chicago capitalises by part of speech; APA
capitalises words of four letters or more in headings but uses sentence case for reference
titles. They disagree about "with", "from" and "over" specifically, which is why two
carefully edited publications can capitalise the same headline differently and both be
right.

## Which to use

**Sentence case** for most web writing, social posts and product interfaces. It reads
faster, it looks less shouty on a phone, and it makes proper nouns stand out because they
are the only capitals.

**Title case** for formal publications, book and article titles inside prose, and anywhere
a house style requires it.

**Neither for ALL CAPS.** It is slower to read — the word shape that helps recognition is
lost — and it reads as shouting in every context where it is not a logo.

## Does case affect SEO?

No. Search engines are indifferent to capitalisation, and URLs should be lowercase for
entirely different reasons — some servers treat paths as case-sensitive, which is a real
source of duplicate pages.

Where case does have an effect is click-through. Sentence case tends to read as
informative and title case as promotional, and which performs better depends on the
audience rather than the algorithm. It is worth testing on your own titles rather than
adopting somebody else's conclusion.

## Consistency is the actual rule

Pick one, write it into your own style notes, and apply it to headings, social posts,
button labels and email subject lines alike. Mixed case across a site is the kind of
detail that makes work look unedited even when nobody can say why.

Three practical notes:

- **Never capitalise for emphasis.** Bold exists.
- **Watch the truncation.** Case does not change where a title is cut —
  [YouTube title length](/blog/youtube-title-length) — but it changes how the fragment
  reads.
- **Check your CMS.** Some themes apply CSS `text-transform`, which capitalises visually
  while the underlying text stays as written. That is fine until the text is used
  somewhere else.

The style guides themselves are the reference when a house style has to be settled: the
[Chicago Manual of Style](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/) and AP's stylebook are
the two most commonly adopted.

:::faq
Q: What is title case?
A: Capitalising the significant words in a heading, with articles and short prepositions
left lowercase. The exact definition of "significant" varies by style guide.
Q: Should I use title case or sentence case?
A: Sentence case for web and social writing; title case for formal publications. The
important part is consistency.
Q: Does capitalisation affect SEO?
A: No. Search engines ignore case. URLs should still be lowercase, for server and
duplication reasons rather than ranking ones.
Q: Why do style guides disagree about title case?
A: They define "major words" differently - by length in AP, by part of speech in Chicago -
so short prepositions are capitalised in one and not the other.
:::
