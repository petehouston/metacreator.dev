There are three ways to screenshot a tweet, and they differ in quality and in honesty. A
phone capture is quick and low-resolution; a desktop capture is sharper; a generated card
is the cleanest and is a mock-up rather than a record. Knowing which one you are looking
at matters.

## How to screenshot a tweet: three methods

**Phone.** Fast, and captures at your screen's resolution — which looks soft the moment
it is scaled up or posted somewhere wider. Crop tightly; the surrounding interface adds
nothing.

**Desktop browser.** Sharper, and you can zoom the page before capturing for a
higher-resolution result. Use the browser's own screenshot tool where it has one rather
than a whole-screen capture.

**A generated card.** Renders the text cleanly at whatever size you need.

badge**, because a drawn card is a mock-up. Every run carries a warning saying so.

## The honesty problem

An image of a post is not evidence. Anyone can produce one that says anything, in seconds,
and the fact that it looks exactly like a real screenshot is precisely the issue.

Two rules that keep you out of trouble:

**Link to the original whenever it exists.** The link is the evidence; the image is the
illustration. This is also more useful to your reader, who can see the replies.

**Never present a generated card as a capture.** Using a drawn card to illustrate a
design, a template or a hypothetical is fine and normal. Using one to claim somebody said
something is fabrication, and it is the reason our tool refuses to draw a verification
tick.

If the original has been deleted, say that it was deleted and, where possible, point to an
archive. "Screenshot from a since-deleted post" is a sentence that costs nothing and
protects everything.

## Making it readable

A screenshot is going into a feed where images are cropped and scaled, so the same rules
apply as to any other image:

[[tool:social-image-resizer]]

- **Crop to the post.** Remove the interface, the sidebar, and your own bookmarks bar.
- **Capture at high resolution**, then downscale. Never upscale.
- **Watch the timeline crop.** X shows a band from the middle of a tall image, so a
  screenshot of a long thread is unreadable until tapped —
  [Twitter image sizes for X](/blog/twitter-image-size).
- **Add alt text.** A screenshot of text is invisible to a screen reader unless you
  transcribe it in the alt field. This is the most commonly skipped step and the least
  defensible.

## Where these images end up

Screenshots of posts are used as social proof, in newsletters, in slide decks and as blog
illustrations. In every one of those contexts the same two questions apply: is it
readable at the size it will be seen, and is it clear whether it is a record or an
illustration.

X's own guidance on embedding posts — the option that is neither a screenshot nor a
mock-up, and preserves the link — is in its
[developer documentation](https://developer.x.com/en/docs/x-for-websites/embedded-posts/overview).
Embedding is the honest default for a website; a screenshot is for places that cannot
embed.

The broader posting mechanics are in
[how to post on X and Threads](/blog/how-to-post-on-x).

:::faq
Q: How do I screenshot a tweet without the clutter?
A: Capture on desktop and crop to the post, or use a generator that draws the card
cleanly at the size you need.
Q: Is it legal to screenshot someone's post?
A: Sharing a public post as commentary is ordinary practice. Passing off a fabricated
card as a real post is not, and altering someone's words is defamation risk regardless of
format.
Q: Why does my screenshot look blurry?
A: It was captured at phone resolution and scaled up. Capture larger and downscale
instead.
Q: Should I embed or screenshot on my own site?
A: Embed where you can - it preserves the link, updates if the post is edited, and makes
clear that it is real.
:::
