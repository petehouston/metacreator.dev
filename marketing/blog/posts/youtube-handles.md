A YouTube handle is the `@name` that identifies your channel — unique across YouTube,
usable in mentions, and the basis of your `youtube.com/@name` URL. It is not the same
thing as your channel name, which can be anything and can duplicate someone else's.

## The rules a YouTube handle has to follow

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Length | 3 to 30 characters |
| Allowed characters | Letters, numbers, underscores, hyphens, full stops |
| Not allowed | Spaces, most symbols, handles that look like a URL |
| Uniqueness | One per channel, across all of YouTube |
| Case | Displayed as typed, but not case-sensitive for uniqueness |

Reference: [YouTube Help on handles](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11585688).

## Choosing one you can keep

A handle is the closest thing a channel has to a permanent public name, so the
selection criteria are boring and correct:

- **Short enough to say out loud.** If you cannot dictate it over a phone call, it is
  wrong.
- **No numbers standing in for letters.** They are misheard and mistyped forever.
- **The same name you use elsewhere**, if you can get it. A creator with the same
  handle on four networks is findable; one with four different handles is not.

## Changing a handle, and what it breaks

You can change your handle, and YouTube limits how often. The important part is what
happens to everything that referenced the old one:

- **Old `@handle` URLs stop working.** Anything printed, linked or embedded with the
  old handle is a dead link.
- **The channel ID does not change.** Feeds, subscribe links and API integrations
  built on the `UC…` are unaffected — which is the argument for building on the ID in
  the first place. See
  [how to find a YouTube channel ID](/blog/find-youtube-channel-id).
- **Mentions do not follow.** Past comments and descriptions that referenced you keep
  the old text.
- **A released handle can be claimed by someone else.** This is the real risk: your
  old audience typing the old name may land on a stranger's channel.

So the honest advice is to change a handle at most once, early, and then never again.

## Handles, channel names and custom URLs

Three different things that people use interchangeably:

- **Handle** — `@yourname`. Unique, changeable, what people search and mention.
- **Channel name** — the display name. Not unique, changeable, can contain spaces.
- **Custom URL** — the legacy `/c/name` form, superseded by handles.

Your handle appears in Shorts, comments, mentions and search, so it is doing more work
than the display name in most places a stranger encounters you.

## Where the handle sits in growth

Nowhere important, and that is worth saying plainly. A memorable handle removes
friction when someone is trying to find you again; it does not get you recommended.
The fields that do are in the [YouTube SEO guide](/blog/youtube-seo).

:::faq
Q: What is the difference between a YouTube handle and a channel name?
A: The handle is a unique @name used in URLs, mentions and search. The channel name is
a display name that does not have to be unique and can contain spaces.
Q: How often can I change my YouTube handle?
A: YouTube limits changes within a rolling period. Treat it as something to get right
once rather than something to iterate on.
Q: What happens to my old YouTube handle if I change it?
A: It is released and can be claimed by another channel. Old links break; your channel
ID and anything built on it keeps working.
Q: How do I check whether a YouTube handle is available?
A: Paste it into the handle availability checker for a definite answer and variant
suggestions if it is taken.
:::
