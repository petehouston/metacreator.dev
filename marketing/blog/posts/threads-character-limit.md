The Threads character limit is 500 characters per post. Text longer than that is not
rejected — it chains into a follow-up post, which is a friendlier behaviour than a hard
cap and quietly changes how the platform reads.

## The Threads character limit in context

| Platform | Limit | Behaviour past it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Threads | 500 | Chains into a follow-up post |
| X | 280 standard | Rejected; longer for premium accounts, collapsed behind "Show more" |
| Instagram caption | 2,200 | Truncated behind "more" |
| LinkedIn | Long | Truncated behind "see more" |

## Writing to 500 rather than to 280

Five hundred characters is roughly a short paragraph: enough for a claim and its
justification, which is more than X allows and much less than a blog post. That middle
size suits a particular kind of writing — a complete small thought.

Two practical habits:

**Do not fill it.** A 500-character post that uses 480 reads as dense in a feed. The
posts that travel are usually well under the cap.

**Place the split yourself.** If you are going over, decide where the break goes rather
than letting the app choose. A chain that breaks between two ideas reads deliberately; one
that breaks mid-sentence reads like an accident.

## Replies are the mechanism

Threads surfaces conversation, which makes a reply the most valuable response a post can
get — more than a like, and more than a repost.

That has a direct effect on how to use the character budget: **spend the last sentence on
something answerable**. Not "thoughts?", which is a request rather than a question, but
something with a real answer that a reader already has.

## Crossposting from X

The tighter limit wins. A 500-character Threads post does not fit on X at all, while a
280-character X post reads as clipped on Threads but works.

If you publish to both, write at X's limit and let Threads carry it — the alternative is
maintaining two versions, which nobody sustains. The full comparison, including what
actually travels on each, is in
[how to post on X and Threads](/blog/how-to-post-on-x) and the counting rules are in
[the Twitter character limit](/blog/twitter-character-limit).

Meta documents Threads' features in its
[help centre](https://help.instagram.com/), which is the reference to check when a limit
changes.

:::faq
Q: What is the character limit on Threads?
A: 500 characters per post. Longer text chains into a follow-up post rather than being
rejected.
Q: Can I write longer posts on Threads?
A: Yes, as a chain. Place the break yourself so it falls between ideas rather than
mid-sentence.
Q: Do links count toward the Threads limit?
A: Links occupy characters as written, unlike X's fixed-length substitution - so a long
URL genuinely costs you space.
Q: Should I post the same text on Threads and X?
A: Write to X's tighter limit if you post to both. Text written for 500 characters does
not fit on X.
:::
