Pinterest SEO is search engine optimisation, not social media marketing. People arrive
with a query, Pinterest matches it against text it has read, and a well-optimised Pin can
surface months or years after it was published — which is the opposite of every other
platform in this catalogue.

## Pinterest SEO runs on four fields

| Field | Seen by users? | Read by Pinterest? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pin title | Yes — roughly the first 40 characters in feeds | Yes |
| Pin description | No, not in feeds | Yes, for relevance matching |
| Board name and description | Yes, on the board | Yes |
| Destination page | On click | Yes |

The second row is the one people get wrong. Pinterest allows up to 800 characters of
description and uses it for matching, but it does not appear in home or search feeds
([Pinterest specs](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/pinterest-product-specs)).
So it should be written as clear prose for a machine, not as a caption for a human.

## What to put where

**Title.** The query, in natural words, front-loaded. Up to 100 characters with roughly
the first 40 shown in feeds, so the important part goes first.

**Description.** A few sentences of plain prose containing the query and its natural
variants. No hashtag walls — Pinterest is not a hashtag platform, and a description of
tags is a description of nothing.

**Board.** Named for the subject, not for a mood. "Small kitchen storage" ranks; "Bits I
like" does not. Board structure is covered in
[Pinterest board strategy](/blog/pinterest-board-strategy).

**Destination.** The page should actually be about the thing the Pin promises. Pinterest
reads it, and so does the person who clicked.

## The image is the ranking signal you cannot fake

Pinterest is visual search, and a Pin that nobody saves does not rank however good its
text is. Two mechanical requirements before any of that matters:

- **2:3 ratio at 1000×1500.** Taller Pins are truncated in the feed, which cuts the call
  to action off the bottom.
- **A text overlay of six words or fewer**, legible at feed-tile size.

## Rich Pins, and why they are worth setting up

A Rich Pin pulls metadata from your page — article, product or recipe details — and makes
the Pin more informative without any extra work per Pin. It depends on markup on your
site being present and valid.

## Finding the keywords in the first place

Pinterest's own search box is the research tool, and it is better than most paid
alternatives for this platform because it reflects Pinterest's users rather than Google's.

**Guided search.** Type a seed term and Pinterest offers coloured tiles of related
qualifiers underneath the search bar. Those are its own suggestions for how people narrow
that query - a ready-made list of long-tail terms with demand attached.

**Autocomplete.** The same principle as everywhere: what Pinterest completes is what
people type.

**The Pins that already rank.** Search your term and read the titles and boards of the top
results. That tells you the vocabulary Pinterest currently associates with the query.

Work outward from a seed into ten or fifteen specific terms, and build a Pin for each
rather than one Pin for the broad term. The broad term is what you eventually win by
covering the narrow ones - the same logic as
[YouTube keyword research](/blog/youtube-keyword-research), on a platform where it works
even more reliably.

## A publishing rhythm that suits the platform

Because Pins are assets rather than posts, the sensible cadence looks different from
social:

- **Several Pins per piece of content.** Different images, different titles, same
  destination. This is normal on Pinterest and not duplication.
- **Publish ahead of the season.** Searches run weeks to months early, so seasonal
  material published in-season has already missed its window.
- **Keep pinning steadily.** Consistency over months beats bursts, because the value of
  each Pin accrues slowly.

## What makes Pinterest different from social

**Longevity.** A Pin can be discovered a year later. This changes the economics: a Pin is
an asset, not a post.

**Volume is rewarded.** Pinning consistently and often works here in a way it does not on
platforms that rank by engagement velocity.

**Seasonality is real.** Pinterest searches run ahead of the season, so material should be
published well before the event it serves.

**Followers barely matter.** Distribution is search-driven, so a small account with
well-optimised Pins competes with a large one.

Pinterest publishes its own guidance for businesses in its
[help centre](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business), and the writing side of it is
[writing a Pin description Pinterest can read](/blog/pinterest-pin-description).

:::faq
Q: Does Pinterest SEO actually work?
A: Yes, and more predictably than social ranking, because Pinterest matches queries
against text it has read rather than ranking by engagement velocity.
Q: Should I use hashtags on Pinterest?
A: No. Write the description as prose containing your keywords. Pinterest is a keyword
search engine rather than a hashtag platform.
Q: How long does it take for a Pin to rank?
A: Weeks to months, and Pins keep surfacing long after publication - which is the main
argument for treating them as assets.
Q: Do board names affect Pinterest SEO?
A: Yes. Board names and descriptions are read, so a subject-specific board name helps
every Pin on it.
