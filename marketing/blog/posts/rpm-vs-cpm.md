RPM vs CPM, in one line: CPM is what advertisers pay per 1,000 ad impressions before
YouTube takes its share, and RPM is what you actually earn per 1,000 video views after
it. RPM is always the lower number, and it is the one that describes your income.

## RPM vs CPM, side by side

| | CPM | RPM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stands for | Cost per mille | Revenue per mille |
| Whose number | The advertiser's | Yours |
| Measured per | 1,000 ad impressions | 1,000 video views |
| Includes YouTube's cut? | No — it is before the split | Yes — it is after |
| Includes non-ad revenue? | No | Yes: memberships, Super Thanks, Premium |
| Counts views with no ad? | No | Yes — every view is in the denominator |

Two structural differences do most of the work. CPM counts only the views where an ad
ran; RPM counts every view. And CPM is measured before YouTube's share, while RPM is
what lands in your account.

That is why a channel can see a healthy CPM and a disappointing RPM at the same time,
and why comparing your RPM to somebody else's CPM produces despair for no reason.

## Where the gap between them goes

Start with a CPM. Subtract:

1. **Views with no ad.** Ad blockers, viewers YouTube chose not to serve, videos with
   limited advertiser suitability.
2. **YouTube's share.** Creators receive 55% of net ad revenue on long-form watch-page
   ads ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72902)).

Then add back:

3. **Everything else.** Channel memberships, Super Thanks, Super Chat, and your share
   of YouTube Premium watch time. All of it is in RPM and none of it is in CPM.

## Which one to track

**Track RPM.** It is the number that describes your business, it includes revenue
streams CPM ignores, and it moves for reasons you can act on.

Use CPM diagnostically. A rising CPM with a flat RPM usually means fewer of your views
are being monetized — worth investigating. A falling CPM across the board in the first
weeks of a calendar year is seasonal and means nothing about your channel.

## What actually moves RPM

| Lever | Effect | Under your control? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Niche and subject | Very large | Yes, at the channel level |
| Audience geography | Large | Indirectly, through subject and language |
| Video length and ad breaks | Moderate | Yes |
| Advertiser suitability | Large when it applies | Yes |
| Season | Moderate, cyclical | No |
| Non-ad revenue streams | Large over time | Yes |

The first row dominates. A channel about business software and a channel about pranks
can differ by a multiple in RPM with identical view counts, which is the reason niche
selection is a financial decision as much as a creative one.

## Before either number exists

Both require Partner Programme membership: 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 qualified
public watch hours in 12 months, or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days
([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851)).

:::faq
Q: What is the difference between RPM and CPM?
A: CPM is what advertisers pay per 1,000 ad impressions before YouTube's share. RPM is
your revenue per 1,000 video views after it, including non-ad income.
Q: Why is my RPM so much lower than my CPM?
A: Because RPM counts every view, including those with no ad, and it is measured after
YouTube's 45% share of ad revenue.
Q: Can RPM be higher than CPM?
A: Occasionally, if a large share of income comes from memberships, Super Thanks or
Premium watch time rather than ads.
Q: Which should I use to estimate earnings?
A: RPM, applied to total views. Using CPM against total views overstates income
substantially.
:::
