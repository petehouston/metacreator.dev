Instagram caption length is capped at 2,200 characters, and roughly the first 125 are
shown before the caption is truncated behind "more". The cap is irrelevant; the fold is
the whole design problem, because everything after it is read only by people you have
already convinced.

## Instagram caption length, in numbers

| Thing | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Maximum caption | 2,200 characters |
| Shown before "more" | ~125 characters, roughly a line and a half |
| Hashtags allowed | 30, and they count toward the 2,200 |
| Bio, separately | 150 characters |

Meta documents the 2,200-character caption limit in its advertising specifications
([Meta Business Help](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/103816146375741)), and the
same field length applies to organic posts. The fold position varies slightly by device
and by how long your username is, which is why it is a range rather than a number.

## Writing for the fold

**The first line is the caption.** Treat everything after "more" as a bonus for people
who tapped.

Three shapes that work in 125 characters:

- **A claim.** "This costs £4 and replaces a £60 gadget."
- **A question with a real answer.** Not "thoughts?" — something answerable.
- **The first half of a sentence that needs finishing.** Used sparingly, before it
  becomes a tic.

What does not work is context. "So I've been thinking a lot lately about…" spends the
entire visible caption on throat-clearing.

## Long captions are not wrong

A long caption is a legitimate format — a story, an explanation, a useful reference — and
they earn saves, which are weighted heavily. The rule is not "be short"; it is that the
first line has to earn the tap.

If you write long captions regularly, two habits help:

- **Break them into short paragraphs.** A wall of text on a phone is skipped regardless
  of quality.
- **Put the payoff before the sign-off.** Most people stop before the end.

## Hashtags and the fold

Hashtags count toward the 2,200 and, more importantly, occupy visible space if you put
them at the top. Put them at the end, or in the first comment — there is no measurable
reach difference between the two, so the decision is purely about how the caption reads.

## What the caption is actually for

Worth stating, because "write a good caption" is advice without a mechanism. A caption
does three jobs, and most posts attempt only the first.

**It tells Instagram what the post is about.** The recommendation system reads text. A
caption that says nothing specific gives it nothing to work with, which affects who the
post is shown to.

**It converts a scroll into a stop.** The visible line is competing with the image above
it and the next post below. This is the job the fold governs.

**It earns the interaction.** Saves come from usefulness; comments come from a question
somebody can actually answer; shares come from a point worth passing on. A caption that
ends without asking for anything usually gets nothing, and a caption that asks for
everything gets ignored.

One post cannot do all three well. Pick the one that matters for that post and write for
it - which, in practice, means deciding whether this is a post to be saved or a post to
be discussed, before you write a word.
