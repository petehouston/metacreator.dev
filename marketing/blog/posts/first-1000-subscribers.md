How long 1,000 subscribers takes is a division problem: subscribers remaining, divided
by your current rate of gain. Everything else — the anecdotes, the "I did it in 30
days" videos — describes somebody else's channel in somebody else's niche at a
different time.

## Do the arithmetic first

[[tool:follower-milestone-countdown]]

The countdown takes your current count and your recent growth rate and projects the
date. It is deliberately unexciting: if you are gaining 40 subscribers a month and you
have 200, the answer is twenty months at the current rate, and knowing that is more
useful than hoping.

What makes it actionable is the second question the projection forces: **what would have
to change for that number to halve?** Usually one thing, and it is rarely "post more".

## Why 1,000 subscribers is the number people care about

It is half of the Partner Programme threshold: 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000
qualified public watch hours in 12 months or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days
([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851)).

[[tool:youtube-partner-program-checker]]

Most channels reach the watch hours before the subscribers, which surprises people. Four
thousand hours is 240,000 minutes — ten videos averaging 24,000 minutes of watch time
apiece. A channel with genuine search traffic accumulates that steadily, while
subscriptions require a viewer to make a decision.

Full detail:
[YouTube monetization requirements](/blog/youtube-monetization-requirements).

## What actually converts a viewer into a subscriber

Not asking. Asking helps at the margin and is worth doing once, briefly, in the middle
of a video. What converts is a viewer thinking *"I want the next one of these"* — which
requires there to be an obvious next one.

Three things that do the work:

**A recognisable subject.** A channel about one thing gets subscribed to; a channel
about whatever the creator felt like this week gets watched and forgotten. The viewer is
predicting your future videos from this one.

**A catalogue.** Someone who liked a video and finds four more like it subscribes far
more often than someone who finds an empty channel. This is why subscriber growth
accelerates - the catalogue does the work.

**Findability.** You cannot convert people who never arrive. Most small channels have a
discovery problem rather than a conversion problem, and the fix is in the
[YouTube SEO guide](/blog/youtube-seo) and
[YouTube keyword research](/blog/youtube-keyword-research).

## The two phases nobody warns you about

Growth to a thousand is not linear, and expecting it to be is what makes people quit at
month four.

**Phase one is flat.** Videos get tens of views, mostly from people you know, and
nothing compounds because there is no catalogue and no signal for YouTube to act on.
This phase lasts longer than anyone likes and it is not evidence that the channel will
fail. It is what the first ten videos look like for almost everyone.

**Phase two is one video.** Almost every channel's growth chart has a step in it - a
single video that found a real query and answered it well, after which everything
behind it got watched too. That video is usually not the one the creator expected, which
is the argument for making enough of them to find out.

The practical implication: judge the channel on whether you are learning between videos,
not on the subscriber count, until you have published enough to have had a fair chance
at the step. Ten videos is a reasonable minimum before drawing conclusions.

## What does not work

- **Sub4sub.** Against YouTube's policies, and the subscribers do not watch — which
  damages the ratio YouTube uses to decide who to show your videos to.
- **Buying subscribers.** Removed when detected, and visible in your engagement figures
  meanwhile.
- **Giveaways for subscriptions.** Attracts people who want the prize. If you run one,
  run it to reward an audience you have rather than to buy one:
  [Instagram giveaway rules](/blog/instagram-giveaway-rules) applies the same logic on
  a different platform.
- **Begging in the first ten seconds.** The viewer has not been given a reason yet.

## Making the ask, once

There is a version of "please subscribe" that works, and it is specific: at the moment
in the video where you have just delivered something useful, say what the next video
will do for them. That is a reason, not a request.

:::faq
Q: How long does it take to get 1,000 subscribers on YouTube?
A: Divide the subscribers you still need by your recent monthly gain. For most channels
publishing consistently, the honest answer is months rather than weeks.
Q: Do I need 1,000 subscribers to make money on YouTube?
A: For ad revenue, yes - it is half the Partner Programme threshold. Sponsorships,
affiliates and your own products require none.
Q: Why are my views growing but not my subscribers?
A: Usually because the channel's subject is unclear, or because there is no obvious next
video. Viewers subscribe to a promise about the future.
Q: Does asking people to subscribe work?
A: Slightly, once, at the right moment - after you have delivered something and while
you tell them what the next video does.
:::
