A YouTube video not showing in search is almost never a shadowban. It is usually one
of five settings that remove the video from a surface outright, and each one takes
under a minute to check. Work through them in this order — they are listed by how
often they turn out to be the cause.

## 1. Visibility is not what you think it is

Unlisted and private videos do not appear in search, ever, for anyone. That sounds
obvious until you remember that scheduled uploads sit as private until their publish
time, and a video published from a phone can default differently from one published
on desktop.

Check: Studio → Content, and read the Visibility column rather than trusting memory.
"Public" is the only value that appears in search.

## 2. Made for kids removes half the surfaces

Marking a video as made for kids is a legal setting, not a stylistic one, and it
disables a long list of features — including some discovery surfaces, comments,
notifications and personalised ads
([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076)).

Channels set this at the channel level and forget it, and then every upload inherits
it. If your videos are not for children, this is worth verifying once at channel
level and once on the video.

## 3. Age restriction

An age-restricted video is not shown to signed-out viewers or to anyone under 18, and
it disappears from several recommendation surfaces. Restriction can be applied by you
in Studio, or applied automatically by YouTube's own review — the second kind is easy
to miss because nothing about the watch page looks different when you are signed in
as the owner.

Check: Studio → Content → the video → Restrictions column.

## 4. It has not finished processing, or it is very new

A freshly uploaded video is not immediately in the search index. High-resolution
processing continues after publication, and indexing takes its own time. For a new
channel it can take noticeably longer.

Before concluding anything, search for the exact title in quotes while signed out. If
the video appears there but not for a broad phrase, nothing is wrong — you are
looking at a ranking outcome, not a suppression.

## 5. The settings you did not know were settings

Category, language, licence and made-for-kids status all sit in the same panel and
all affect where a video can appear. A video with the wrong language set can be shown
to the wrong audience, or fail to appear for the audience it was made for.

## When a YouTube video is not showing in search for any of these reasons

Then the video is visible and simply not ranking, which is a different problem with
different fixes. The honest diagnosis order:

| Symptom | What it means | Where to look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Impressions, no clicks | Title and thumbnail are not earning the click | [Title length](/blog/youtube-title-length) |
| Clicks, no watch time | The first thirty seconds break the promise | The video itself |
| No impressions at all | YouTube has nobody to show it to yet | [YouTube keyword research](/blog/youtube-keyword-research) |

That last row is the common one for small channels, and it is not a punishment. A
video about a phrase nobody searches has no search demand to capture; the fix is
choosing a phrase people type before making the next one.

## About the word "shadowban"

YouTube does not operate a hidden penalty that suppresses a compliant video while
telling you everything is fine. What exists is a set of documented settings and
policies that limit distribution, and they are all inspectable. If a video is not
showing, one of them is set, or the video is simply not being chosen — and the
difference between those two is exactly what the five checks above establish.

:::faq
Q: Why is my YouTube video not showing in search results?
A: Most often visibility, made-for-kids status, age restriction, recent upload
processing, or an unintended setting like language or licence. Check all five before
assuming a ranking problem.
Q: How long does it take for a YouTube video to appear in search?
A: Usually hours, sometimes longer for a new channel. Search the exact title in
quotes while signed out to confirm it is indexed.
Q: Is there such a thing as a YouTube shadowban?
A: Not as a hidden penalty on compliant videos. What exists are documented settings
and policies that restrict distribution, and every one of them can be inspected.
Q: Does made for kids affect search?
A: Yes. It disables a range of features and discovery surfaces. If your content is
not for children, check the setting at both channel and video level.
:::
