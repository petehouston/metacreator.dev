YouTube publishes no monetization badge, so asking whether a YouTube channel is
monetized has no single definitive public answer. What the public page does carry is a
set of signals that, taken together, are usually conclusive — and a set that people
mistake for evidence and should not.

## The signals that do mean something

**Membership and Super Thanks buttons.** A "Join" button on a channel, or Super Thanks
on its videos, requires Partner Programme membership. This is the strongest public
signal there is.

**Merchandise shelves and product shelves.** Also gated behind the programme.

**Ads on the videos.** Seeing an ad on a channel's video is good evidence, with
caveats: ads may not show on every playback, your ad blocker changes what you see, and
Premium viewers see none at all. Absence of an ad is not absence of monetization.

**The channel's own claims.** Creators say so, in descriptions, in videos, and in
community posts. Not proof, but not nothing.

## The signals that mean nothing

- **Subscriber count.** A channel can have a million subscribers and no monetization —
  through a policy issue, a region where the programme is unavailable, or choice.
- **View count.** Same reasoning.
- **A verification tick.** That is about identity, not money.
- **Sponsorship segments in videos.** Brand deals are negotiated directly and require
  no programme membership at all. A channel can be earning well and be outside YPP
  entirely.

That last one matters more than it looks. Plenty of channels earn most of their income
from sponsors, affiliates or their own products, none of which YouTube mediates. "Not
monetized" and "not earning" are different claims.

## Why you would want to know

Usually competitive research: if you are considering a niche, the mix of monetized and
unmonetized channels in it tells you something about whether the niche supports ads at
all. Some subjects reliably attract limited advertiser suitability, and knowing that
before committing a year to it is worth an hour of checking.

## What determines whether a channel can be monetized

The programme requires 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 qualified public watch
hours in the last 12 months or 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days,
along with policy, region, verification and AdSense conditions
([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851)).

## Checking your own channel

For your own channel there is no ambiguity: YouTube Studio has a Monetization tab that
states your status, and each video carries a monetization icon showing whether ads are
running, limited, or off. A yellow icon means limited advertiser suitability on that
video — a per-video judgement that can be appealed, not a channel-level penalty.

If videos are earning nothing and you assumed something was wrong with distribution,
check those icons before investigating anything else. The same instinct applies to
reach problems generally, where the cause is usually a setting rather than a mystery —
see [YouTube video not showing in search](/blog/youtube-video-not-showing-in-search).

:::faq
Q: Can you tell if a YouTube channel is monetized?
A: Not with certainty from outside, but membership buttons, Super Thanks, product
shelves and ads on videos are strong indicators. YouTube publishes no badge.
Q: Does seeing an ad prove a channel is monetized?
A: It is good evidence, though ads do not run on every playback and Premium viewers
see none. Not seeing one proves nothing.
Q: Can a channel earn money without being in the Partner Programme?
A: Yes, and many do. Sponsorships, affiliate links and their own products need no
programme membership.
Q: How do I check my own channel's monetization status?
A: The Monetization tab in YouTube Studio states it directly, and each video shows an
icon for whether ads are running, limited or off.
:::
