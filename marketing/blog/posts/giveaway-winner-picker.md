The problem with picking a giveaway winner is not fairness — it is *provable* fairness. A
draw done off-camera and announced afterwards looks exactly the same as one that was fixed,
and no amount of assurance closes that gap. A giveaway winner picker that publishes a
reproducible seed does.

[[tool:giveaway-winner-picker]]

## How a verifiable giveaway winner picker works

1. **Close entries and freeze the list.** Export or screenshot the entries so the pool is
   fixed and visible.
2. **Publish the seed before drawing**, or publish it with the result. A seed is the number
   that determines the random sequence — the same seed and the same list always produce the
   same winner.
3. **Draw.**
4. **Announce the winner, the entry count and the seed together.**

Anyone can then re-run the draw and get your answer. That is the whole mechanism, and it
converts "trust me" into something checkable.

## Handling the awkward cases

**Duplicate entries.** Decide before you draw whether one person can enter twice, and say
so in the rules. Multiple comments from one account is the usual case, and either policy is
fine as long as it was published in advance.

**Invalid entries.** People who did not follow the rules should be excluded before the
draw, not after the result is inconvenient.

**The winner does not respond.** Publish a deadline in the rules — 48 hours is common — and
say what happens next. Drawing a replacement is fine if the rules said you would.

The rules to publish, and the entry mechanics that attract the right people rather than
prize-hunters, are in [Instagram giveaway rules](/blog/instagram-giveaway-rules).

## What not to do

**Do not screenshot a scrolling list and claim you stopped at random.** Nobody can check
it, and the accusation that you stopped where you wanted is unanswerable.

**Do not draw before entries close.** Obvious, and it happens - usually because the
announcement was scheduled and the deadline slipped.

**Do not exclude someone after the draw.** If they broke the rules, they should have been
excluded before. Removing an inconvenient winner afterwards is the single fastest way to
lose an audience's trust, and it is always noticed.

**Do not run the draw somewhere nobody can see the method.** A tool that produces a name
with no reproducible basis is a coin flip you are asking people to take on faith - which
is exactly what the seed exists to replace.

## Why it matters beyond the giveaway

A disputed draw is remembered, and the accusation is impossible to disprove after the fact.
Publishing the seed costs one line in the announcement post and removes the entire category
of problem.

There is a second reason: it is a small, visible demonstration that you do things properly.
Audiences notice.

## After the draw

Expect a follower drop — prize-hunters leave, which is the mechanism working. The
measurement worth doing is engagement rate a month later against a month before, not
follower count:

If it fell and stayed down, the giveaway bought an audience that does not care —

Platform rules apply on top of your own: Instagram requires organisers to publish official
rules and eligibility and to state that the promotion is not associated with Instagram
([Instagram Help](https://help.instagram.com/179379842258600)).

:::faq
Q: How do I pick a giveaway winner fairly?
A: Freeze the entry list, draw at random using a published seed, and announce the winner,
the entry count and the seed together so anyone can reproduce the result.
Q: What is a seed in a random draw?
A: A number that determines the random sequence. The same seed and the same list always
produce the same result, which is what makes a draw checkable.
Q: Should duplicate entries count?
A: Either policy works, as long as it was published in the rules before entries opened.
Q: What if the winner does not reply?
A: Publish a response deadline in the rules and say what happens if it passes. Drawing a
replacement is fine when it was announced in advance.
:::
