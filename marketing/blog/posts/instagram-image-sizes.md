The Instagram image size you want is 1080 pixels on the short edge, in one of three
ratios: 4:5 for feed posts, 1:1 for square, 9:16 for Stories and Reels. Everything
else on Instagram is one of those three with a different crop applied on top.

## Instagram image size, by placement

| Placement | Ratio | Export | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Feed, portrait | 4:5 | 1080×1350 | The most screen space a single post can take |
| Feed, square | 1:1 | 1080×1080 | Safest for a tidy grid |
| Feed, landscape | 1.91:1 | 1080×566 | Smallest footprint — avoid unless the image demands it |
| Carousel | Same ratio for every slide | 1080×1350 | Mixing ratios crops the whole set to the first slide |
| Reels | 9:16 | 1080×1920 | Interface covers the edges |
| Reels cover | 9:16, plus a 1:1 grid crop | 1080×1920 | One image, two crops |
| Stories | 9:16 | 1080×1920 | Same safe-zone problem as Reels |
| Profile picture | 1:1 | 320×320 or larger | Displayed as a circle |

## Why 4:5 is worth more than square

A vertical feed gives each post a fixed width and whatever height its ratio implies.
A 4:5 post is therefore 25% taller than a square one at the same width — a quarter
more screen, a quarter longer in view before the thumb moves on.

The trade-off is the grid: portrait posts are cropped to squares in the profile grid,
so a composition with the subject at the very top or bottom looks decapitated there.
Compose for 4:5 with the subject inside the central square and both views work.

## Carousels: the ratio is set by the first slide

Instagram applies the first slide's ratio to the entire carousel. Add a square slide
to a 4:5 carousel and it is letterboxed or cropped; the mistake is invisible in your
editor and obvious in the feed.

## Reels covers are two crops of one image

The cover appears full-height at 9:16 when the Reel is playing, and as a cropped tile
in your profile grid. Those two crops share very little area, which is why so many
covers have the title cut in half on the profile.

## Stories: the frame is not the safe area

A Story is 9:16, but the top carries the profile row and progress bars, and the bottom
carries the reply field and any stickers. Text placed in those bands is covered on
some devices and clear on others.

## The grid crop is a second composition

Everything in a profile grid is square, whatever ratio it was posted at. That gives
every portrait post two lives: the feed view it was composed for, and the square crop
a visitor sees when they land on your profile.

Channels that care about the grid solve this in one of two ways. Either they compose
4:5 with the subject inside the central square - so the crop takes only empty space -
or they accept the crop and treat the grid as a texture rather than a gallery. What
does not work is centring a subject in the bottom third of a portrait image and then
wondering why the profile shows a chin.

The same logic applies to Reels, which appear in the grid as a vertical tile rather
than a square one, and to carousels, where only the first slide is ever shown there.

## File size and format

Export as JPEG for photographs and PNG for anything with flat colour or text. Meta
publishes its own creative specifications for the placements it sells advertising on
([Meta Business Help](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/103816146375741)), and
organic posts follow the same geometry.
Instagram recompresses everything, so an oversized upload is downscaled by their
algorithm rather than yours — which is the usual cause of "my image looks soft on
Instagram but fine on my laptop".

## The whole-network view

Instagram's ratios are the same ratios everywhere else, which is the useful thing
about learning them once — 9:16 is a Story, a Reel, a TikTok and a Short. The full
cross-network reference is

:::faq
Q: What is the best Instagram image size?
A: 1080×1350 at 4:5 for feed posts. It takes the most vertical space of any allowed
ratio, which means more attention per post.
Q: Why does Instagram crop my photo?
A: Because the ratio does not match the placement. A 16:9 image in a 4:5 slot has to
lose its sides, and a carousel takes its ratio from the first slide.
Q: Does Instagram reduce image quality?
A: It recompresses every upload. Exporting at the correct dimensions, already
optimised, gives a better result than uploading a very large file.
Q: What size is an Instagram profile picture?
A: Square, 320×320 or larger, displayed as a circle. Keep the subject centred - the
corners are always cut off.
:::
