Use three to five YouTube hashtags, in the description, all relevant to the video.
YouTube displays up to three of them above your title, ignores every hashtag on a
video carrying more than 60, and says over-tagging may remove a video from search
altogether. More is not better here — it is a documented risk.

## The rules, from YouTube's own documentation

| Rule | What YouTube says |
| --- | --- |
| Display | "Up to three hashtags that are considered most engaging will appear by your video title" |
| Ceiling | "If a video or playlist has more than 60 hashtags, we'll ignore each hashtag on that content" |
| Spaces | "Hashtags don't contain any spaces" — join words: `#twowords` |
| Over-tagging | "May result in the removal of your video from your uploads or from search" |

Source: [YouTube Help on hashtags](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6390658).

Read the second row again: past 60, you do not get diminishing returns, you get zero.
And the fourth row is not a ranking penalty in the vague sense — it is removal.

## Where they go

In the description. Hashtags placed in the title also work, but they consume title
characters that are worth more as words — and
[title length](/blog/youtube-title-length) is already the tightest constraint you
have.

Put them at the very bottom of the description. YouTube will pull up to three of them
to render above the title, so their position in the text costs you nothing and keeps
your visible lines clean.

## Choosing three YouTube hashtags that do something

YouTube picks which three to display, based on which it considers most engaging. You
influence that by giving it a short list where every entry is genuinely about the
video:

- **One broad**: the subject category. `#sourdough`
- **One specific**: what this video is actually about. `#sourdoughstarter`
- **One contextual**: the format, series or audience. `#baking101`

That is it. A fourth and fifth are fine if they are equally relevant. A thirtieth is
a signal that none of them are.

## Hashtags are not tags

Constantly confused, entirely different:

| | Hashtags | Tags |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where | In the description, visible | In Studio, hidden |
| Clickable | Yes, they open a hashtag page | No |
| Documented effect | Modest discovery; three shown | "Minimal role", except misspellings |
| Limit | 60 before all are ignored | A field cap in Studio |

YouTube is blunt about tags: they "play a minimal role in your video's discovery"
([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/146402)). If you want to
read the tags on someone else's video anyway — they are useful as vocabulary
research — that is [how to see the tags on any YouTube video](/blog/how-to-see-tags-on-a-youtube-video).

## What hashtags will not fix

A hashtag does not rescue a video nobody clicks. It is a small discovery surface and
a navigational convenience, sitting several rungs below the title, the thumbnail and
whether people keep watching. If a video is underperforming, the hashtags are not the
reason, and the [YouTube SEO guide](/blog/youtube-seo) covers the fields that are.

:::tip One hashtag habit worth having
Keep a consistent series hashtag across a run of videos. It gives a returning viewer
a way to find the rest of the series in one tap, which is the one job hashtags do
genuinely well.
:::

## Other networks work differently

Hashtag behaviour does not transfer. Instagram allows up to 30 per post; LinkedIn's
convention is around three; TikTok treats them as topical signals in a caption with
its own limit. The per-network differences are in
[how to use hashtags](/blog/how-to-use-hashtags).

:::faq
Q: How many hashtags should I use on YouTube?
A: Three to five, all relevant, in the description. YouTube displays up to three of
them above the title.
Q: What happens if I use too many hashtags on YouTube?
A: Past 60 on one video, YouTube ignores all of them. Over-tagging may also result in
the video being removed from search or from your uploads.
Q: Do hashtags in the YouTube title work?
A: Yes, they render as links - but they use characters that are worth more as words,
and titles are cut short on most surfaces anyway. The description is the better place.
Q: Are YouTube hashtags the same as tags?
A: No. Hashtags are public, clickable and live in the description. Tags are hidden
metadata that YouTube says plays a minimal role in discovery.
:::
